# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 – Adult Education Survey (Bar chart + Pie chart)

Source: `task1.png`

**Question:** The charts below show the results of a survey of adult education. The first chart shows the reasons why adults decide to study. The pie chart shows how people think the costs of adult education should be shared. Write a report for a university lecturer, describing the information shown below. (150+ words, 20 minutes)

This is a **mixed chart** task (bar chart + pie chart).

---

## 1. How to approach it

**Read the data (2 minutes):**

- **Bar chart:** reasons adults study. The values add up to more than 100% (161%), so respondents could choose several reasons.
  - Top two: interest in the subject (40%) and gaining qualifications (38%).
  - Middle group: about 20–22% (helpful for current job 22%, promotion 20%, enjoy learning 20%).
  - Bottom two: changing jobs (12%) and meeting people (9%).
- **Pie chart:** who should pay for each course.
  - Individual 40%, employer 35%, taxpayer 25%.
- **Link between the two charts:** personal motives (interest, qualifications) are the most common reasons, and the individual is also the party most people think should pay the most. Job-related reasons are mid-ranked, and employers are expected to pay a large share (35%).

**Things to remember:**

- No opinions and no explanations of why, only description and comparison.
- Use the present tense, because the survey has no dates.
- Report the data without copying the question wording.
- Group the reasons into 3 levels instead of listing all 7 bars.

---

## 2. Structure (4 paragraphs, ~190 words)

| Paragraph | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Intro | Paraphrase: the survey results on why adults study and who should fund courses |
| Overview | (1) Interest and qualifications were by far the most popular reasons; meeting people and changing jobs were the least. (2) Individuals were thought to carry the largest share of costs, taxpayers the smallest |
| Body 1 | Bar chart: top group (40%, 38%), middle group (22%, 20%, 20%), bottom group (12%, 9%) |
| Body 2 | Pie chart: individual 40%, employer 35%, taxpayer 25%, with a link back to the bar chart |

---

## 3. Band 8.0 sample answer

The bar chart illustrates the reasons why adults choose to study, while the pie chart shows people's opinions on how the cost of each course should be divided.

Overall, personal interest and gaining qualifications were the most common motivations for adult education, whereas practical benefits such as changing careers or socialising were far less significant. In addition, the majority of respondents believed that individuals and employers should bear most of the cost, with taxpayers contributing the smallest share.

Looking at the reasons for studying in more detail, 40% of adults enrolled out of interest in the subject, closely followed by 38% who wanted to obtain qualifications. Work-related factors came next: 22% said the course was useful for their current job, and 20% hoped to improve their promotion prospects, the same proportion as those who simply enjoyed learning. By contrast, only 12% studied in order to change jobs, and meeting people was the least popular reason, at just 9%.

Turning to funding, 40% of respondents felt that individuals should pay for their own courses, which was the highest figure. Employers were expected to cover a further 35%, so that these two groups together would account for three-quarters of the total cost. Only a quarter of those surveyed thought that the taxpayer should contribute.

*(About 215 words.)*

---

## 4. Why this reaches band 8

- **Task Achievement:** a clear overview with the main trends from both charts, accurate figures, and key features highlighted rather than every number listed.
- **Coherence and Cohesion:** logical grouping (top, middle, bottom) and a clear link between the two charts, with varied linkers (*closely followed by, came next, by contrast, turning to*).
- **Lexical Resource:** precise, varied vocabulary (*motivations, bear most of the cost, contribute, the same proportion as*) with no repeated words.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** complex sentences (*whereas*, *so that*, *which was...*, *the same proportion as those who...*), accurate and varied.
