# IELTS Writing Study

## Project purpose

This folder is my IELTS Writing study project. When I paste an essay, letter or report, act as my IELTS writing coach: identify the task/essay type, check it against the outline below, mark it on the four criteria (band estimate each), list my repeated errors, and show a corrected or improved version of weak sentences. Keep feedback concise and specific.

---

## 1. Overview

IELTS Writing is 60 minutes, two tasks. Task 2 counts double, so spend about 20 minutes on Task 1 and 40 on Task 2.

| | Task 1 | Task 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 20 min | 40 min |
| Minimum words | 150 | 250 |
| Weight in score | 1/3 | 2/3 |
| Academic | Describe a graph, chart, table, process or map | Essay |
| General Training | Letter | Essay |

**Four scoring criteria (25% each):**

1. **Task Achievement / Task Response** – answer every part of the question; Task 1 needs an overview, Task 2 needs a clear position.
2. **Coherence and Cohesion** – logical paragraphs, one main idea each, clear linking.
3. **Lexical Resource** – range and precision of vocabulary, collocations, few spelling errors.
4. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** – mix of simple and complex sentences, mostly error-free.

**Ground rules:** always plan for 2–3 minutes, write in formal style (no contractions), never copy the question wording, and leave 3 minutes to proofread.

---

## 2. Roadmap (12 weeks)

Four phases from foundations to timed practice; study 5 days a week, about 60–90 minutes a day. Take a diagnostic essay in week 1 and a full timed test in weeks 6, 9 and 12.

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Foundation | 1–2 | Test format, band descriptors, sentence grammar, paragraph structure, diagnostic test | Baseline band + list of top 5 weak points |
| 2. Task 1 skills | 3–4 | Reading data, overview writing, comparison language; (GT) letter tone and bullet coverage | 6 Task 1 answers, one per question type |
| 3. Task 2 skills | 5–8 | Idea generation, the 5 essay types, topic vocabulary (education, technology, environment, health, work, crime) | 1 essay per type, then 4 more on mixed types |
| 4. Exam mode | 9–12 | Timed full tests, error log review, polishing grammar and vocabulary | 4 full tests (60 min) with self-marking and feedback |

**Weekly rhythm**

- Day 1–2: learn one question type (outline, model answer, language)
- Day 3: plan ideas only (5 questions, 5 minutes each)
- Day 4: write one full answer, untimed
- Day 5: rewrite using the error log, then add 10 new vocabulary items

**Target check:** Band 6.0 needs a clear answer and basic organisation; band 7.0 needs a clear overview or position, full development and varied grammar. Compare self-marking against the four criteria every week.

## 3. Task 1 – Academic (report, 150+ words, 20 min)

Seven question types share one skeleton: **Introduction → Overview → Detail 1 → Detail 2**, four short paragraphs (about 170–190 words). Describe and compare the data; never give opinions or reasons.

### Universal outline

1. **Introduction (1–2 sentences):** paraphrase the question: what the graph shows, where, and when.
2. **Overview (2 sentences):** the 2–3 biggest trends or differences, with no numbers. Essential for band 6+.
3. **Detail paragraph 1:** group the first set of data, with figures, and compare.
4. **Detail paragraph 2:** group the second set, with figures, and compare.

### The seven types

| Type | What to do | Body paragraph grouping | Key language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Line graph | Describe trends over time | Group lines that behave alike, or split the time period in two | rose / fell / fluctuated / peaked at / levelled off |
| Bar chart | Compare categories (and time) | Highest vs lowest categories, or by year | was higher than / twice as much as / the largest share |
| Pie chart | Compare proportions (often two charts) | Biggest segments first, then smaller ones; or chart 1 vs chart 2 changes | accounted for / made up / a quarter of |
| Table | Pick key figures, do not list every number | Highest values, then lowest values; or by row groups | the highest figure / ranked first / by contrast |
| Mixed charts | Two different visuals (e.g. pie + table) | One paragraph per visual, link them in the overview | in contrast to / while / whereas |
| Process | Describe stages in order, usually passive | Stages 1–N split into two halves | First / Next / Following this / Finally; is heated, is then filtered |
| Map | Describe changes between two times, or plan vs reality | Changes in the north/centre, then south/other areas | was replaced by / was converted into / to the east of |

### Type-specific tweaks

- **Line / bar / table over time:** overview = overall direction plus the highest or most notable item. Use correct tenses (past for past dates, future for forecasts).
- **Pie charts:** overview names the largest and smallest portions. With two pies, state what increased and decreased between them.
- **Process:** overview states the number of stages, the start and the end ("The process has six stages, beginning with ... and ending with ..."). Use sequencing words, not numbers. Present simple passive.
- **Maps:** overview = the main type of change (urbanisation, expansion, removal). Use compass directions and prepositions of place.

### Sentence templates

- Intro: *The line graph illustrates how ... changed in ... between [year] and [year].*
- Overview: *Overall, X increased significantly, whereas Y declined. Z remained the highest throughout.*
- Detail: *In [year], X stood at 20%, before climbing sharply to 45% in [year], overtaking Y.*

## 4. Task 1 – General Training (letter, 150+ words, 20 min)

The letter gives a situation and three bullet points: cover all three, each in its own paragraph or clearly developed part. Choose the tone from who you write to.

| Letter type | Written to | Tone | Opening / closing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Formal | Manager, company, council, landlord | Polite, impersonal, no contractions | Dear Sir or Madam, ... Yours faithfully, (name unknown) / Dear Mr Smith, ... Yours sincerely, |
| Semi-formal | Neighbour, teacher, colleague, someone you know slightly | Polite but friendly | Dear Mr / Ms Brown, ... Kind regards, |
| Informal | Friend, relative | Friendly, contractions and phrasal verbs fine | Dear Anna, ... Best wishes / Take care, |

### Universal outline (4 paragraphs)

1. **Opening (1–2 sentences):** state why you are writing ("I am writing to complain about...", "I am writing to ask for...", "I just wanted to tell you...").
2. **Bullet 1:** details, reason or explanation (2–3 sentences).
3. **Bullet 2:** details (2–3 sentences).
4. **Bullet 3 + closing:** details, then the action you expect or a friendly sign-off ("I look forward to hearing from you").

### Purpose templates

- **Complaint:** problem → what happened (facts, dates) → what you want done (refund, replacement, apology).
- **Request / enquiry:** reason → specific questions or needs → thanks and deadline.
- **Apology / explanation:** apologise once → explain what went wrong → offer a solution.
- **Invitation / suggestion / news (informal):** greeting and news → the details asked for → invitation or plan, then reply soon.

**Watch for:** wrong tone (too casual in a formal letter), missing a bullet point, and long openings such as "I hope you are well" in a formal letter.

## 5. Task 2 – Essay (250+ words, 40 min)

Five essay types. Identify the type from the instruction words, then use its outline. Every essay has 4–5 paragraphs (about 270–300 words): **Introduction, Body 1, Body 2, (optional Body 3), Conclusion.** Each body paragraph follows **PEEL**: Point (topic sentence) → Explain → Example → Link back to the question.

### How to recognise the type

| Type | Question usually says | Your job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Opinion (Agree/Disagree) | "To what extent do you agree or disagree?" | Give and defend your own position |
| 2. Discussion (+ opinion) | "Discuss both views and give your opinion." | Cover both views fairly, then say which you prefer |
| 3. Advantages / Disadvantages | "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" | Weigh both sides and judge |
| 4. Problem / Solution (Causes / Effects) | "What problems... what can be done?" | Name problems or causes, then realistic solutions |
| 5. Two-part question | Two separate questions, e.g. "Why is this? Is it a positive or negative development?" | Answer each question in its own body paragraph |

### Universal introduction and conclusion

- **Introduction (2–3 sentences):** (1) paraphrase the topic, (2) state your answer or thesis, (3) optionally outline what each body paragraph will cover.
- **Conclusion (2 sentences):** restate your position in different words and summarise the 2 main points. No new ideas.

### 1. Opinion essay

Pick one side and keep it consistent. Aim for "strongly agree" or "strongly disagree" with clear reasons, or a partial agreement if it is honest.

1. **Intro:** paraphrase + *I completely agree / I partly agree that...*
2. **Body 1:** your strongest reason (PEEL, with an example)
3. **Body 2:** your second reason (PEEL, with an example)
4. **Body 3 (optional):** the opposing view acknowledged, then refuted (*Although some argue..., this overlooks...*)
5. **Conclusion:** restate position + summary

### 2. Discussion essay (both views + your opinion)

1. **Intro:** paraphrase + *This essay will discuss both views before giving my own opinion* + your thesis
2. **Body 1:** View A with 1 developed reason and an example
3. **Body 2:** View B with 1 developed reason and an example
4. **Conclusion:** your opinion (*In my view, the second argument is more convincing because...*) + summary

If the question asks for your opinion, state it in the introduction as well, so the essay is clear throughout.

### 3. Advantages / Disadvantages essay

Two forms: *Discuss the advantages and disadvantages* (cover both) or *Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?* (you must give a judgment).

1. **Intro:** paraphrase + thesis (*Although there are some drawbacks, I believe the benefits outweigh them.*)
2. **Body 1:** 2 advantages (or the main one), explained and exemplified
3. **Body 2:** 2 disadvantages (or the main one), explained and exemplified
4. **Conclusion:** which side is stronger and why

### 4. Problem / Solution (and Cause / Effect)

1. **Intro:** paraphrase + *This essay will outline the main causes/problems and suggest some solutions.*
2. **Body 1:** problem 1 or cause 1, explained, with an effect or example; then problem 2 if time permits
3. **Body 2:** a solution for each problem; say who should act (government, schools, individuals) and why it works
4. **Conclusion:** summarise the problems and the solutions

Match each solution to a problem named in Body 1 to keep the essay coherent.

### 5. Two-part question

1. **Intro:** paraphrase + a short answer to both questions
2. **Body 1:** answer question 1 fully (reason + example)
3. **Body 2:** answer question 2 fully (reason + example)
4. **Conclusion:** summarise both answers

Do not mix the two questions in one paragraph.

### Paragraph template (PEEL)

*One major reason is that ... (Point). This is because ... (Explain). For instance, in [country/study/situation], ... (Example). Therefore, ... (Link).*

### Common mistakes

- Writing a general essay instead of answering the exact question
- Unclear or changing opinion
- Memorised, off-topic examples
- Long introductions and no real development of ideas
- Fewer than 250 words, or no conclusion

## 6. Language bank

| Purpose | Useful phrases |
| --- | --- |
| Giving opinion | In my view / I firmly believe that / It is my contention that |
| Adding | Furthermore / In addition / Moreover |
| Contrasting | However / Nevertheless / Whereas / Despite the fact that |
| Giving reasons | This is because / Owing to / Due to the fact that |
| Giving examples | For instance / A case in point is / Take ... as an example |
| Result | As a result / Consequently / Thus |
| Conclusion | In conclusion / To sum up / All things considered |
| Trends (Task 1) | rose sharply / declined gradually / remained stable / reached a peak of |
| Approximation (Task 1) | approximately / just under / nearly / roughly |
| Hedging | tends to / is likely to / may well / can be argued |

### Habits that raise your score

- Vary sentence openings: do not start every sentence with "This" or "The".
- Use 2–3 complex sentences per paragraph (*Although..., ...*, *which...*, *if...*).
- Use topic-specific collocations rather than rare words you are unsure of.
- Keep an **error log**: write each repeated mistake (articles, plurals, tenses) with the corrected version.

## 7. Self-check before you submit

**Task 1**

- [ ] Did I paraphrase the question in the introduction?
- [ ] Is there a clear overview (Academic) or are all 3 bullets covered (GT)?
- [ ] Did I group the data logically and compare, not list?
- [ ] Are tenses and units correct?
- [ ] 150+ words?

**Task 2**

- [ ] Did I identify the essay type and answer every part of the question?
- [ ] Is my position clear in the introduction and conclusion?
- [ ] Does each body paragraph have one main idea with an explanation and example?
- [ ] Did I use linking words naturally, not in every sentence?
- [ ] 250+ words, and 3 minutes left to check spelling, articles and plurals?

**Practice rule:** one essay of each type, then redo the weakest type until the self-marked score improves in two criteria.
