# CORRECTED VERSION OF YOUR ESSAY (your ideas, fixed language)

Nowadays, many different kinds of music from around the world can be heard everywhere. Music has a hidden power: it can influence our emotions, make life more beautiful and, most importantly, connect people across the globe. While the traditional music of a country is very important, I believe international music also plays a significant role in everyone's spiritual life.

Music is something we cannot live without. Love stories and the meaningful values of life can all be delivered to people through beautiful melodies. A moving song can win over even the most discerning listeners, and it can change people's feelings from hatred to love or from sadness to happiness. In the darkest moments of life, music can make people feel more confident and help them love life again. Indeed, several studies have shown that people feel happier when they listen to music. Many songs also carry meaningful messages that spread around the world. For instance, Michael Jackson used songs such as "Heal the World" to speak on behalf of vulnerable children. That is why music is so important in our lives.

In my view, traditional music and international music both play a significant role, and neither is more important than the other. On the one hand, the traditional music of a country makes young people feel proud of their nation. It also reminds the next generation of their country's history and of how their ancestors defended it so that we can live in peace today. On the other hand, international music helps people understand the cultures of other countries. Some people even learn foreign languages through songs, and local musicians can borrow new styles to enrich their own music.

In conclusion, music is an essential part of life because it shapes our emotions and brings people together. Traditional music should be preserved as a valuable part of a nation's culture, but we should also remain open to international music.

# MODEL ESSAY (Band 7.5–8 target, ~320 words)

Music in its many forms, from folk songs to global pop hits, is found in almost every human society. In my view, people need music because it supports their emotional well-being and brings communities together. As for traditional and international music, I believe neither is more important than the other, although traditional music deserves special protection because it is more easily lost.

The main reason humans need music is its powerful effect on emotions. Listening to a favourite song can reduce stress, lift a low mood or give people the energy to keep going during difficult periods. This is why many hospitals now offer music therapy to patients recovering from surgery or suffering from depression. Music also has an important social function. Singing the national anthem at a football match or dancing together at a wedding creates a shared experience that strengthens the bonds between people, regardless of their age or background.

Turning to the second question, traditional music matters because it carries a nation's identity. Folk songs often record historical events, local values and even dialects that might otherwise disappear. In Vietnam, for example, Quan ho folk singing has been recognised by UNESCO as cultural heritage precisely because it preserves centuries-old customs. Nevertheless, international music has its own value. Global hits allow young people from different countries to share common interests, and they often motivate learners to improve their English. Moreover, many modern artists blend local instruments with international styles, which introduces traditional sounds to a worldwide audience. In this way, the two types of music support rather than compete with each other.

In conclusion, music is a basic human need because it improves our emotional health and unites people. Although both traditional and international music are valuable, governments and schools should make a particular effort to keep traditional music alive, since, unlike global hits, it cannot survive on commercial popularity alone.
